After Turkey, I didn't intend to stay put for long, so I set off for Northeastern Europe with my good friend [Carlo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/carlo-annicchiarico-785162175/).

We spent about a month working remotely together, starting in Vilnius. Since it was November, temperatures were already below freezing, so we often sought refuge in cozy co-working spaces well-stocked with herbal teas and warming drinks.

The central market and the Old Town were particularly interesting. We also took a trip to Trakai Castle to immerse ourselves in the medieval atmosphere and learn a bit more about the local history. Naturally, we couldn't pass up the chance for a memorable night of electronic music at [Kablys](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Kablys/@54.6730144,25.2749288,17z/data=!4m15!1m8!3m7!1s0x46dd9537b40d9775:0x2334f35661f7066f!2sKablys!8m2!3d54.6730144!4d25.2775091!10e1!16s%2Fg%2F11fp8p93_m!3m5!1s0x46dd9537b40d9775:0x2334f35661f7066f!8m2!3d54.6730144!4d25.2775091!16s%2Fg%2F11fp8p93_m?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MDgwNS4xIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D).

About ten days later, we headed to Riga, where we found plenty of snow and temperatures dropping below -10°. We sampled everything from market street food, including raw herring to medieval style taverns.

Fun fact: all the locals there spoke to me in their own language, mistaking me for a local.
