In 2025, after leaving Australia, I visited Bali for the first time, and to be completely honest, the weather was terrible. We didn't have much time, and we encountered several days of thunderstorms and flooding in the island's more developed areas. Plus, since it was my first time there, I had to handle errands like opening a local bank account, getting a SIM card, and signing various permits for the land we had leased.

Fortunately, we managed to fully enjoy our final days before leaving by exploring Jatiluwih, Ubud, and Seminyak. I returned in 2026 to check on our investment and spent about two months working remotely in Kedungu, a fantastic village near Tabanan, where the locals brought me joy from day one to the very end. The beaches on that side of the island are better suited for surfers, but you only need to head to places like Sanur, Nusa Dua, Blue Lagoon Beach, or Uluwatu to find breathtaking white sands and crystal-clear water.

After more than two months, I feel more than confident driving anywhere necessary, from the chaotic traffic of Canggu to the dirt roads in the mountains of Tabanan.

I've read plenty of blog posts bashing Bali for overtourism and a lack of authenticity, but that seems to me a very superficial view. Bali is home to over five million permanent residents; naturally, the main tourist hubs lack authenticity. It would be like going to Venice and complaining that it's a tourist trap. Wake up, guys: just avoid the crowded areas if you don't want to be in the middle of scene. The mountains and the north of the island, for instance, are still wonderfully wild.

I will always be grateful to the locals I met during those months. Wonderful, truly sharp people.
