For my 33rd birthday, I was given a long weekend in Warsaw. Since I was born in early February, we arrived to find it snowing and temperatures sitting at minus 11. We stayed at a hotel right in the Old Town, centrally located and easy to cover on foot, and right after checking in we headed straight to some milk bar spots for goulash, raw herring and the local pilsner.

Over the next few days we visited the science museum, the library, the vodka museum, the hedge maze, the street food market and took several walks along the river. The guide in the vodka museum was incredibly prepared and funny, the tour was incredibly pleasant, the history totally crazy and unbelievable and the tasting delicious.

The city center is compact enough to get around by walking, which makes it easy to stumble into things you weren't planning on.

I had been reading about Poland's economy for a while. It's been consistently reported as the fastest growing in Europe, and walking around Warsaw you can feel it. The city feels well managed and technologically up to date, with a strong enough identity that it doesn't feel generic, but open to foreigners as well. That combination is harder to pull off than it sounds.

Fun fact: I developed a mouth abscess mid trip and couldn't find a pharmacy willing to hand over an antibiotic without a prescription. Annoying, but it didn't slow me down much.
